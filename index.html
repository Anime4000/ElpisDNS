Choose Your Gun
Somewhere between your keyboard and the rest of the world, a machine is asked a very simple question: where is this? Whoever answers that question owns your internet. They can log it. They can sell it. They can decide that today, for reasons nobody wrote down, this particular door is closed. Most people never find out who is answering. You are about to.

Everything on this table is loaded and encrypted end to end — DNS over HTTPS, DNS over TLS — so the answer arrives sealed and the question never travels in the open. Pick the one that fits the job: a local resolver for speed, an international one for the doors that keep mysteriously closing, DNS64/NAT64 if your network has already moved on from IPv4. The prettiest gun in the drawer is rarely the one that gets you home.

The Blacklist
Every name on it earned its place. Ads, trackers, malware, phishing kits, and the quiet little domains that exist for no reason other than to follow you home — the resolver simply declines to tell you where they live. Nothing to install, nothing to configure: it is already running on the endpoint you picked above.

These are the lists doing the work. They are not ours to hoard, so the subscription URLs are right here — take them for your own AdGuard Home, Pi-hole or Blocky.

Bootstrap DNS servers
A chicken and egg problem: to reach an encrypted resolver by name, something has to resolve that name first. These plain resolvers exist for that single moment, and for nothing else. Most clients call the field bootstrap.

Running your own resolver?
Every entry on this page comes out of a single file: dns.json. Copy a block, change the values, open a pull request. No build step, no framework, no meeting. CONTRIBUTING.md has the whole ritual, and it fits on one screen.
